# References for EC HW2 (new papers)

This page lists 22 new sources for EC HW2: **21 peer-reviewed papers and 1 magazine column** ([13], not peer-reviewed). None of them is cited in either version of our proposal. Each one backs one design choice in our MongoDB, Neo4j or comparison work, so the report can cite it where that choice is made.

ORES (Halfaker & Geiger 2020) was first listed here as [16]. It is cited in our proposal draft, so it is now [P3] ("already cited, not counted"), next to [P1] and [P2].

## How we found and checked the papers
1. Date of the check: 2026-10-24 (UTC).
2. How we found them. This was **not** a keyword search in a database. We screened 35 new papers:
   - 22 came from the candidate list in the build plan. Claude Code (an AI tool) suggested this list.
   - 13 came from the reference lists of papers we read ("snowballing") and from earlier reading.
   - We also re-checked 3 proposal papers ([P1]–[P3]) for the gap table.
3. How we checked them. For every paper we checked the title, all authors, venue, year, pages and DOI on the Crossref API. Then we read the abstract (Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex) or the full text (publisher or author PDF). We used Crossref, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex to check papers, not to find them.
4. The "Verified" column links the pages we opened. A paper we could not verify is left out.
5. We kept 22: 21 peer-reviewed papers and 1 magazine column. We left out 13: 1 that we could not read in full (Potthast 2008), and 12 that are real but less tied to a design choice. All 13 are on Crossref. See [Checked but left out](#checked-but-left-out).
6. The style is IEEE. The numbers [1]–[23] are fixed, so the report can use them. The number [16] is no longer used (ORES is now [P3]).

Words used below: **Mongo Q1–Q5** and **Cypher Q1–Q5** are numbered as in the spec (sections 3 and 4). "Identity revert" means a later edit brings the page back to an earlier exact version (same SHA-1).

**What the gap table shows**
1. No row has all six columns.
2. The papers that measure time (or survival time) until a revert ([1], [6], [P1]) use old history, not a live stream.
3. The system that runs live (ORES [P3]) does not measure time to revert.
4. Revert graphs ([6], [14]) are built from dumps only.
5. No paper on Wikipedia reverts compares database engines. The database paper [17] has no Wikipedia data and no document store.

**A close source that is not a paper.** Wikimedia now builds a daily MediaWiki History table from event streams (T424350, facts W13–W16). Its team found that "10–50% of reverts fall outside 48 hours depending on wiki" (T426469). This is close to our question, so the report must not say that nobody has measured it. But this table is in Wikimedia's Data Lake, and we found no public download on the pages we read. It also gives one coarse share for its own pipeline. It is not a public study of how wrong a live view is after each wait time.

**Numbers to compare with ours (report, Motivation and Results).**
- [1] measured survival time: the time until a revision is next changed, not the time until an identity revert. Its median for all content was 90.4 minutes (2003). The well-known 2.8 minutes is only for the 3,574 mass deletions (vandalism, 0.58% of the revisions). Compare our median T (`history_median_T_h`) with the 90.4 minutes.
- [6] found a median survival of 9.7 minutes (MD5 reverts, 2006).
- [2] found about 70% of reverts within 1 hour (compare `history_T_within_1h_pct`).
- Then say why the numbers can differ:
  - The definitions are not the same (survival time vs time to revert).
