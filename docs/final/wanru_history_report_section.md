## MediaWiki History: Snapshot Reconciliation, Time Travel, and Conflict Graph

MediaWiki History provides a corrected, retrospective view of Wikipedia revisions, whereas EventStreams exposes changes as they occur. Comparing the two sources allowed us to measure how the apparent history of a fixed revision cohort changed as later reverts became available. The history cohort contains 295,016 English Wikipedia revisions created from September 8 through September 10, 2026. In the 2026-09 snapshot, 19,858 of these revisions were known to have been reverted. After loading the 2026-10 snapshot, the count increased to 20,925. Thus, the later snapshot added a net 1,067 reverted revisions to the same cohort rather than merely adding newer edits.

The second snapshot was processed as one Delta Lake `MERGE` commit. Across all included revisions, the operation inserted 5,358,769 new records and changed 40,721 existing labels: 40,683 became reverted, 36 became unreverted, and two retained the same label but changed reverter or timing information. Within the fixed history cohort, 1,072 revisions became reverted, five became unreverted, and one had another label change. These results show that a historical dump is not permanently final; subsequent snapshots can revise the state of records that were already present.

The stream provided an independent check of these changes. The earlier pipeline identified 2,970 exact late reverts of September revisions after the first snapshot cutoff. Of these, 2,967 appeared as newly reverted in the second snapshot, a confirmation rate of 99.9%. For the separate October live cohort of 279,466 revisions, stream and 2026-10 dump labels agreed for 99.86% of revisions. They classified 17,752 revisions as reverted and 261,323 as not reverted in both sources, while 66 were stream-only and 325 were dump-only. The stream therefore recovered 98.2% of the reverts recorded by the later dump. The remaining differences reflect source semantics and exact-revert detection rather than a simple pipeline failure.

Delta Lake time travel preserves both knowledge axes without overwriting earlier answers. On the snapshot axis, `fact_revert_label` version 1 represents the 2026-09 load and version 2 represents the 2026-10 load, allowing the 19,858-to-20,925 change to be reproduced directly. On the stream knowledge-time axis, the number of known reverted revisions in the live cohort increased from 17,040 as of October 24 to 17,818 as of October 27. The 778 newly known rows appeared a median of 2.84 days after their original edits. Commit metadata identifies versions by snapshot or as-of value, and the Delta change feed retains the rows whose labels changed.

We also modeled the history cohort as a Neo4j property graph to examine relationships that aggregate counts cannot show. The graph contains 37,546 `Editor` nodes, 165,415 `Page` nodes, 295,013 `EDITED` relationships, and 13,269 `REVERTED` relationships. Three of the 295,016 history revisions lacked an editor key and therefore could not form `EDITED` edges. A two-edge Cypher traversal identified 462 mutual-revert editor pairs on 397 pages. The most concentrated page, `PB&J_Otter`, contained 10 warring editors, nine mutual pairs, and 34 revert actions among 39 human edits.

Community detection added an important qualification to the edit-war interpretation. Louvain produced 28,615 communities, but only 1,191 contained at least two editors; 27,424 were isolated editors with no revert relationship. The largest community contained 564 editors and 661 revert actions, yet 69.1% of its `REVERTED` relationships touched ClueBot NG. It is therefore better interpreted as a hub-and-spoke patrolling structure than as a community in which every member fought every other member. The Neo4j graph uses the EC HW2 20-day follow-up definition, whereas the lake's E(L) results use a four-day horizon, so their counts are complementary but are not combined. In addition, a mutual revert records reciprocal actions but does not by itself establish malicious behavior.

## Locality-Sensitive Hashing of Edit Comments

We applied PySpark MinHashLSH to identify repeated edit-summary patterns without comparing every pair of comments. After excluding revert actions, empty or short comments, and comments with fewer than four words, the analysis retained 91,092 edits containing 49,140 distinct cleaned texts. Each text was represented as a set of five-character shingles, transformed with binary `HashingTF`, and compared at a minimum Jaccard similarity of 0.60. On an exact validation sample of 4,000 texts, five hash tables recovered 96.11% of the 2,162 true near-duplicate pairs while completing the approximate join in 0.8 seconds, compared with 190.8 seconds for 7,998,000 exact pair comparisons. Increasing from five to twenty hash tables improved recall only from 96.11% to 96.16%, demonstrating diminishing returns.

Across the complete input, LSH returned 420,629 near-duplicate pairs involving 9,988 texts in 1,419 connected groups. Wan-Ru Hsiao manually reviewed 80 sampled candidate pairs: 40 stratified across four similarity bands and 40 drawn from distinct near-duplicate groups. Seventy-seven pairs described the same underlying edit pattern, for an estimated precision of 96.2% with a Wilson 95% confidence interval of 89.5% to 98.7%. The band-stratified sample achieved 40 of 40, while the group-based sample achieved 37 of 40.

Repeated summaries were more common among edits not reverted within four days than among reverted edits: 72.56% versus 24.63% shared an exact or near-duplicate summary. This result does not imply that repetition prevents reversion. Many non-reverted comments were routine templates for category moves, reference maintenance, or automated edits. Moreover, the export intentionally balances reverted edits with a sample of non-reverted edits, so it cannot estimate population prevalence. Short or empty comments are excluded, comments are imperfect proxies for content changes, and MinHashLSH remains probabilistic.
